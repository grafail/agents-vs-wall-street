Agents of S.T.O.C.K.

An AI analyst that forecasts company results, and shows its work · Agents vs Wall Street, 16 Aug 2026

The idea: the AI is the analyst, the code is the calculator. The AI reads the company's filings, does its own research, and forms an opinion. But it never writes the final number: plain, tested code does the arithmetic, keeps the AI's influence inside a measured limit, and double-checks everything before a number reaches the spreadsheet.

What makes this architecture strong

The AI does the hard workIt reads 1,139 company documents, plans its own web research and weighs the evidence. A person runs one command and reviews the result.
Every number is explainedEach figure comes with a worksheet showing which part is data, which is math, and which is the AI's opinion, with sources for everything.
The AI is kept on a leashBefore trusting the AI, the system measures how wrong its own statistics usually are. The AI may only move a number within three quarters of that error.
Cheap to run, so run it oftenHeavy work is cached and reused. A practice run costs cents, the real run a few dollars, so we could test all day.
Works with any modelWhich AI models to use is a settings choice, not code: any model on OpenRouter or OpenAI, mixable in one run. There are three jobs to assign: a cheap fast model for the reading and research, one or several models as the forecasting panel (when panel members disagree, the system automatically trusts them less), and a strong model for the Wall Street check. Swapping any of them is a one-line settings change.
Rich data, clearly sourcedHistorical numbers come from the official record: the supplied corpus of 1,139 SEC filings, UK regulatory announcements, earnings-call transcripts and investor slides. Fresh context comes live from web and news search (DuckDuckGo), Yahoo Finance market data, company investor-relations pages and official statistics releases. Wall Street expectations come from figures the companies themselves publish, then Yahoo Finance as backup. Every fact keeps its exact quote, source and a trust level that settles conflicts.
It fails safelyAn empty cell scores worst possible, so there is a ladder of backups. Something sensible always ships, and anything unusual is loudly flagged for review.

How every number is made: the worksheet

This five-step recipe is the heart of the system. All 12 submitted figures are built this way, and the run report shows the filled-in worksheet for each one:

[D]  anchor      start from what the company actually reported last time   (quoted from the filing)
[M]  baseline    apply a simple statistical forecast                        (the method that proved best on this company's past)
[L]  judgment    let the AI adjust it, within its measured limit            (every opinion needs a source, or it counts for nothing)
[M]  check       compare with Wall Street: justify the gap, or move closer  (the only step where Wall Street's number is allowed in)
[M]  ship        run the safety checks, then write the cell                 (if any step failed, the next backup takes over, flagged)

[D] data · [M] math · [L] the AI's judgment. In one live run the AI pushed a forecast below zero; the safety checks refused it, a flagged backup shipped instead, and reading that worksheet led us to a better method. The worksheet is also how we debug.

The main choices, and why

Freedom only where it helpsThe AI is free where judgment matters (what to research, what to believe). It has no freedom where precision matters (arithmetic, units, file format).
Estimate first, peek laterThe AI forecasts without seeing Wall Street's number, so it cannot just copy it. Only afterwards is it confronted: justify the difference with evidence, or move closer.
Rehearse before trustingEvery statistical method is tested on the company's own past first. The one with the smallest misses wins, and its miss size becomes the AI's adjustment limit.
Whole company at onceThe AI forecasts all of a company's numbers in one sitting, so related figures (like profit and earnings per share) stay consistent with each other.
Prepare early, run lateReading and research are done and saved during the day. The final run reuses them, so it is fast and does not depend on the venue wi-fi.

Honest notes

Things we tried, changed, or got wrong

  • Started with a big data library, replaced it within the hour: a few small tools were easier to trust.
  • Guessed the wrong "key document" for one company; the pipeline found the right one by itself.
  • A published Wall Street figure sneaked into the AI's reading pile mislabeled as company guidance. We sealed the leak, then reused that figure properly.
  • With too little history, the system quietly ignored the AI's opinion entirely. Fixing that uncovered four cousins of the same bug.
  • Forecasting each number separately let profit and earnings per share contradict each other. Now the AI sees the whole company at once.

Still weak, and we know it

  • The AI may half-remember Wall Street's expectations from its training. We disclose this rather than pretend otherwise.
  • Two identical runs can give somewhat different answers. Asking several models helps, but does not remove it.
  • The "mood" adjustments use sensible fixed sizes, not measured ones. The hard limit is the safety net.
  • Some companies report so rarely that our rehearsals rest on only a few examples.
  • The free market-data feed we compare against is the part we trust least.
