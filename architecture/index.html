Agents of S.T.O.C.K.

An AI analyst that forecasts company results, and shows its work · Agents vs Wall Street, 16 Aug 2026

The idea: the AI is the analyst, the code is the calculator. The AI reads the company's filings, does its own research, and forms an opinion. But it never writes the final number: plain, tested code does the arithmetic, keeps the AI's influence inside a measured limit, and double-checks everything before a number reaches the spreadsheet.
1 · Read & researchCode loads the extracted, quoted facts for each company. A small roaming AI agent then searches the web and news on its own (competitor results, economic data, fresh headlines) and writes a short list of findings, each with its source.
2 · Blind estimateOne structured call per company: the AI sees the history, the company's own guidance, the rehearsed statistical baselines and the research findings, and answers a fixed questionnaire: a range for each number plus labeled opinions, every opinion with a source. It is never shown Wall Street's number here.
3 · Street checkCode fetches Wall Street's expectation. If ours differs, the AI is challenged: justify the gap with the evidence, or move closer. Its verdict is a blend weight, applied by code.
4 · Verify & shipCode runs the safety checks (units, sizes, signs, guidance range). If anything fails, the backup ladder supplies the next best number, flagged. The workbook cells are filled, re-verified, and the full audit report is written.

The main choices, and why

Freedom only where it helpsThe AI is free where judgment matters (what to research, what to believe). It has no freedom where precision matters (arithmetic, units, file format).
Estimate first, peek laterThe AI forecasts without seeing Wall Street's number, so it cannot just copy it. Only afterwards is it confronted: justify the difference with evidence, or move closer.
Rehearse before trustingEvery statistical method is tested on the company's own past first. The one with the smallest misses wins, and its miss size becomes the AI's adjustment limit.
Whole company at onceThe AI forecasts all of a company's numbers in one sitting, so related figures (like profit and earnings per share) stay consistent with each other.
Prepare early, run lateReading and research are done and saved during the day. The final run reuses them, so it is fast and does not depend on the venue wi-fi.

The AI's questionnaire

The heart of stage 2. The AI is never asked "what will the number be?". Instead, for every metric it must fill in a fixed form, and anything outside the form is rejected:

a rangepessimistic / central / optimistic adjustment to the statistical baseline. A wide range means the AI is unsure, and the system automatically trusts it less.
momentumis the business heating up or cooling down compared with what the history alone shows? cold, cooling, neutral, warming or hot.
guidance styledoes this management usually lowball its own forecasts, hit them, or overpromise?
surprise skewif the reported result surprises, which direction is more likely?
reasons & sourcesevery answer above must come with its reasoning written first and at least one source. An opinion without a source counts for exactly nothing.

What makes this architecture strong

The AI does the hard workIt reads 1,139 company documents, plans its own web research and weighs the evidence. A person runs one command and reviews the result.
Every number is explainedEach figure comes with a worksheet showing which part is data, which is math, and which is the AI's opinion, with sources for everything.
The AI is kept on a leashBefore trusting the AI, the system measures how wrong its own statistics usually are. The AI may only move a number within three quarters of that error.
Cheap to run, so run it oftenHeavy work is cached and reused. A practice run costs cents, the real run a few dollars, so we could test all day.
Works with any modelWhich AI models to use is a settings choice, not code: any model on OpenRouter or OpenAI, mixable in one run. There are three jobs to assign: a cheap fast model for the reading and research, one or several models as the forecasting panel (when panel members disagree, the system automatically trusts them less), and a strong model for the Wall Street check. Swapping any of them is a one-line settings change.
Rich data, clearly sourcedHistorical numbers come from the official record: the supplied corpus of 1,139 SEC filings, UK regulatory announcements, earnings-call transcripts and investor slides. Fresh context comes live from web and news search (DuckDuckGo), Yahoo Finance market data, company investor-relations pages and official statistics releases. Wall Street expectations come from figures the companies themselves publish, then Yahoo Finance as backup. Every fact keeps its exact quote, source and a trust level that settles conflicts.
It fails safelyAn empty cell scores worst possible, so there is a ladder of backups. Something sensible always ships, and anything unusual is loudly flagged for review.

Where to look, per judging question

Forecasting approachStart from last year's real number, apply the statistical method that proved best on this company's own past, let the AI adjust a little (with sources), then face Wall Street's number.
Model qualityOpen any metric in the run report and you can follow the whole calculation line by line: what was data, what was math, what was the AI's call.
Data approachFilings, transcripts and slides from the frozen corpus for history; DuckDuckGo web/news, Yahoo Finance and IR pages for fresh evidence; company-published consensus before any aggregator. Trust order: filings, then IR and official statistics, then market-data feeds, then news (news alone can never move a number).
Validation & reliabilityAutomatic checks on units, sizes and signs. A backup ladder means no cell is ever left empty. 176 automated tests. A review checklist before upload.
Agent harnessOne command runs everything. The diagram is drawn from the code itself. Every tool call and model call is logged with a timestamp.
Tooling & ergonomicsPurpose-built search, extraction and backtesting tools, plus the audit report that turns each number into a readable worksheet.

Honest notes

Things we tried, changed, or got wrong

  • Started with a big data library, replaced it within the hour: a few small tools were easier to trust.
  • Guessed the wrong "key document" for one company; the pipeline found the right one by itself.
  • A published Wall Street figure sneaked into the AI's reading pile mislabeled as company guidance. We sealed the leak, then reused that figure properly.
  • With too little history, the system quietly ignored the AI's opinion entirely. Fixing that uncovered four cousins of the same bug.
  • Forecasting each number separately let profit and earnings per share contradict each other. Now the AI sees the whole company at once.

Still weak, and we know it

  • The AI may half-remember Wall Street's expectations from its training. We disclose this rather than pretend otherwise.
  • Two identical runs can give somewhat different answers. Asking several models helps, but does not remove it.
  • The "mood" adjustments use sensible fixed sizes, not measured ones. The hard limit is the safety net.
  • Some companies report so rarely that our rehearsals rest on only a few examples.
  • The free market-data feed we compare against is the part we trust least.

Tech stack

Data: historyFrozen corpus of 1,139 documents (SEC filings, RNS announcements, earnings-call transcripts, investor decks). Deterministic narrowing (per-metric synonym search + strong-number line gate) → batched small-model parses → typed Fact records with verbatim quote, canonical units, source doc and trust tier, committed as JSON artifacts.
Data: liveddgs (web + news verticals), yfinance (fundamentals, estimates), requests+trafilatura for page extraction. All behind a read-through disk cache keyed on hash(tool, args) with fetch timestamps; hit/live logged per call. Consensus: corpus-published compiled figures first, yfinance mapping (magnitude-gated) as fallback.
AI modelsOpenAI-compatible via OpenRouter or OpenAI, all env-configured: MODEL_SMALL (extraction, labeling, research scout at low reasoning effort), ESTIMATOR_PANEL (N provider:model members called in parallel, structured outputs, deterministic aggregation: median quantiles, majority-vote labels, disagreement widens the uncertainty band), MODEL_BIG (reconciler). This run: Qwen3.8 + MiniMax M3 panel, DeepSeek v4 reconciler.
PipelinePython 3.13 (uv-locked), LangGraph StateGraph (7 nodes, companies run concurrently), pydantic models end to end incl. structured LLM outputs, pydantic-settings config. 176 pytest tests, LLM/network mocked, real corpus files as fixtures.
Own toolingA walk-forward backtester that picks the best statistical method per metric and sets the AI's adjustment cap; validation gates and the never-blank backup ladder; a workbook writer that refuses to touch anything but the three answer cells. And the report generator: every run's JSON audit trail is rendered into a browsable HTML report, with a worksheet per number, a pre-upload review checklist, pass/fail chips for every check, and per-company pages. It is how we debugged the system all day and how a judge can inspect any number in seconds.
To reproduce: uv sync · keys in .env · prepare: uv run python -m pipeline.run --prepare --all · final run: uv run python -m pipeline.run --forecast --all → 4 workbooks, a timestamped log of every step, and the audit report.
